\section*{S3. Exact rank proof and tie convention}
Best rank is existential:
\[r_i^{\min}=1+\sum_{j\ne i}\ind\{\ell_j>u_i\}.\]
Worst rank is pessimistic:
\[r_i^{\max}=1+\sum_{j\ne i}\ind\{u_j\ge\ell_i\}.\]
Possible membership is $r_i^{\min}\le k$ and guaranteed membership is $r_i^{\max}\le k$. The strict/non-strict distinction controls boundary inclusion in the contraction analysis.

\section*{S4. Safe-screening proof detail}
Let $G=\{i:r_i^{\max}\le k\}$ and $Z=\{i:r_i^{\min}>k\}$. To insert $g\in G$, a size-$k$ list omitting it contains an $h$ with $u_h<\ell_g$, and replacement weakly increases every scenario-wise selected mass. If $i\in Z$ is selected, at least $k$ competitors satisfy $\ell_j>u_i$, so some omitted $j$ strictly dominates $i$ in every scenario. Each such exchange strictly increases every scenario-wise selected mass; because the family of size-$k$ lists is finite, cycling is impossible. Therefore an optimum exists containing $G$ and excluding $Z$ for any minimization objective coordinatewise nonincreasing in scenario-wise selected mass.

\[R_t^{\mathrm{abs}}(x)=V_t^\star-\sum_i p_i^t x_i,\qquad \min_{x:\sum_i x_i=k}\max_tR_t^{\mathrm{abs}}(x).\]

\section*{S7. Secondary SIPP diagnostic and claim boundary}
A separate descriptive diagnostic compares the three Top-25 lists with a fixed 17-occupation target set. The scale-only list contains one target with outgoing SIPP support and captures 50.05\% of target-origin weighted outgoing mass. Baseline composite and relative minimax each contain two supported targets and each capture 66.39\%. These are descriptive overlaps only; they do not estimate out-of-sample regret, worker transition probability, AI causality, or program success.
