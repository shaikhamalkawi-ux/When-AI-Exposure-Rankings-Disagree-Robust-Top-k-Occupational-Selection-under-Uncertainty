\section*{S1. Input and scenario register}
The decision layer uses three frozen normalized exposure channels: AI Occupational Exposure (AIOE) \cite{Sfelten}, Microsoft user-goal, and Microsoft AI-action/nonphysical \cite{Stomlinson}. Employment scale is May 2025 OEWS employment share \cite{Sbls}, and the five occupation-level skill descriptors are O*NET 30.3 Importance values \cite{Sonet}. The construction follows transparent composite-indicator practice \cite{Soecd}.

Let $a_i^{(c)}\in[0,1]$ denote frozen normalized exposure for channel $c$ and $z_{im}\in[0,1]$ the frozen normalized O*NET value for skill $m$. The five skills are Critical Thinking, Active Learning, Learning Strategies, Active Listening, and Speaking. For construction $w$ with retained set $M_w$,
\[
h_i^{(w)}=\frac{1}{|M_w|}\sum_{m\in M_w}z_{im},\qquad q_i^{(c,w)}=a_i^{(c)}\bigl(1-h_i^{(w)}\bigr),\qquad p_i^t=s_iq_i^{(c,w)}.
\]
The equal-five construction uses all five skills; each of the five leave-one-out constructions removes exactly one skill. Thus the 18 primitive priority vectors are the Cartesian product of three exposure channels and six constructions. The upstream normalized values are treated as frozen inputs to this decision-layer paper and are not re-estimated here. The priority box is $\ell_i=\min_t p_i^t$, $u_i=\max_t p_i^t$; the point core is $c_i=p_i^{\mathrm{AIOE,equal-five}}$, hence $\ell_i\le c_i\le u_i$.

\section*{S3. Exact rank proof and tie convention}
Best rank is existential:
\[r_i^{\min}=1+\sum_{j\ne i}\ind\{\ell_j>u_i\}.\]
Worst rank is pessimistic:
\[r_i^{\max}=1+\sum_{j\ne i}\ind\{u_j\ge\ell_i\}.\]
Possible membership is $r_i^{\min}\le k$ and guaranteed membership is $r_i^{\max}\le k$. The strict/non-strict distinction controls boundary inclusion in the contraction analysis.

\section*{S4. Safe-screening proof detail}
Let $G=\{i:r_i^{\max}\le k\}$ and $Z=\{i:r_i^{\min}>k\}$. Order the items in $G$ by nonincreasing lower bound and insert them in that order. If the next $g\in G$ is absent from a size-$k$ optimum, at most $k-1$ competitors satisfy $u_j\ge\ell_g$, so the list contains an $h$ with $u_h<\ell_g$. Any guaranteed item $g'$ inserted earlier has $\ell_{g'}\ge\ell_g$ and hence $u_{g'}\ge\ell_{g'}\ge\ell_g$, so it cannot be chosen as $h$. Replacing $h$ by $g$ therefore preserves all earlier guaranteed insertions and weakly increases every scenario-wise selected mass.

After all $G$ items have been inserted, suppose a selected $i\in Z$ remains. At least $k$ competitors satisfy $\ell_j>u_i$, so at least one such $j$ is omitted. Replacing $i$ by that $j$ strictly increases every scenario-wise selected mass. Because the family of size-$k$ lists is finite, strict exchanges cannot cycle. If a $Z$ item remained, another strict exchange would exist; therefore the process terminates at an optimum containing all $G$ and no $Z$. This establishes the existence claim for every objective coordinatewise nonincreasing in scenario-wise selected mass.
